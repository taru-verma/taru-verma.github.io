About

I'm a final-year Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Michigan, advised by Prof. Todd Austin. My research focuses on architecture-aware GPU performance analysis and kernel optimization, particularly for cryptographic workloads such as big-integer arithmetic, elliptic-curve cryptography, and zero-knowledge proofs.

Previously I've done research on CPU performance analysis, secure processor design, and side channels. I've interned at Apple and Intel Labs with a focus on microarchitecture security and transient execution attacks and defenses. I completed my Master's degree in Computer Engineering from the University of Michigan and my Bachelor's degree from the University of Texas at Dallas.

I'm seeking industry roles in GPU performance engineering, kernel engineering, and computer architecture research starting in early 2027. I would be glad to connect about interesting opportunities over LinkedIn or email.

Research

Computer Science and Engineering, University of Michigan

Present
  • High-performance GPU cryptography: Developed novel GPU-friendly big-integer arithmetic and cryptographic primitives to achieve 3x speedup over state-of-the-art GPU implementations by targeting warp divergence, register usage, async memory operations, and hand-tuned PTX sequences for optimized SASS (in progress)
    • Implemented and evaluated floating-point and Tensor Core-accelerated big-integer cryptography with custom PTX sequences and characterized tradeoffs between arithmetic throughput, representation overhead, and register pressure
  • GPU Performance Analysis: Systematic performance characterization of zero-knowledge proofs, elliptic-curve cryptography, and big-integer arithmetic across several NVIDIA GPU generations to identify performance and scalability limiters across the cryptographic computing stack (ZKProphet, IISWC 2025)
  • Covert Channels over Randomized Caches: Constructed noise-resilient stochastic covert channels on caches with randomized address-to-set mappings to bypass randomization-based cache defenses in the literature and applied them to Spectre attacks (Stochastic Channels on Randomized Caches at SEED 2022)
  • Workload Analysis for Irregular Memory Accesses: Conducted CPU performance characterization with Top-Down Microarchitecture Analysis (TMA) for applications with irregular memory access patterns and developed LLVM passes to identify and extract different types of indirect memory accesses for driving a hardware/software co-designed prefetcher (contributed to Prodigy at HPCA 2021)
  • Secure Hardware Design: Assisted in extending the Morpheus (ASPLOS 2019) secure processor with efficient runtime code and code-pointer encryption, performed security analysis for BROP attacks, and led FPGA bring-up for DARPA FETT Bug Bounty Program (contributed to Morpheus II at HOST 2021 and Hot Chips 33)
